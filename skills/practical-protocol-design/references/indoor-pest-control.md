# Indoor Pest Control Protocols

Domain depth for household pest problems where the user wants a treatment plan
rather than a literature summary. The transferable structure is the same for any
small indoor arthropod: **map stages to vulnerabilities, find the lever that hits
the most stages, measure with a bait assay, and treat the substrate rather than
the adults.**

## The general shape

Every control decision comes from three columns: which stage, where it lives,
what actually kills it. Adults are the visible stage and the least useful target
except through traps, because one surviving female restarts the infestation.
The vulnerable stages are the egg (desiccation) and the larva (feeding and
mobile, therefore exposed to whatever reaches its zone).

Spraying adults is almost always the wrong move: no residual, only hits what is
flying at that moment, and leaves the breeding substrate untouched.

## Fungus gnats (Bradysia, Sciaridae)

| Stage | Duration at 22-24 C | Where | Killed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Egg | 4-7 days | Soil cracks near surface, in moist peat media | Desiccation. **No dormant stage** |
| Larva | 2-3 weeks, 4 instars | Top few mm to cm, deeper when surface is dry | Drenches, nematodes, predatory mites, starvation |
| Pupa | 4-6 days | In medium near surface | Poorly. Barrier or disturbance only |
| Adult | 7-10 days | Pots, windows, lights | Traps, exclusion |

Generation 17 days at optimum, typically 3-4 weeks. Overlapping indoors, so all
stages are present simultaneously and there is no clean generation gap.
Females lay 50-300 eggs. Adults do not feed.

**Moisture is the primary lever.** Larval survival peaks around 60-75%
gravimetric moisture; below ~59% and above ~80% emergence drops sharply. Eggs
desiccate within days. So "dry the top 2-3 cm" works by killing eggs and young
larvae outright, not by waiting them out, and there is no egg bank to outlast.

**Pupae are the gap.** They do not feed, so gut-delivered actives miss them, and
their cuticle is thicker and more tanned. Nicotine and similar contact poisons
may reach them but that is unproven for this species; plan on blocking emergence
rather than killing pupae. Because pupae sit at or near the surface and push
through toppings, disturbing the top centimetre after a dry-down has real value.

## Barrier layers: what the trials actually show

Physical toppings are the most over-recommended control. The evidence:

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Cloyd et al. 2007 (J. Econ. Entomol. 100:1353) | Sand layers at 3.1 and 6.3 mm had **no effect** on emergence or oviposition; pupae protruded through and 4th instars migrated through |
| Kansas State MF2937 | "Not even a 3.1 mm layer of sand was shown to be effective" |
| NZ Plant Protection 54:43 (2001) | 22 mm river sand reduced counts vs control, but larvae and pupae were found **inside** the topping |
| HortScience 49:905 (2014) | Only the thickest (3.18 cm) layer of the smallest (2 mm) aggregate cut emergence, and larvae still migrated through |

Consequences: sand does not block gnats, and **fine sand is worse than useless**
because capillary wicking keeps it damp to the surface, which is the preferred
oviposition condition, and it crusts and cracks into ideal laying sites. A
"combination" of fine plus coarse reintroduces the wicking. Coarse material
(3-8 mm) works only insofar as it breaks capillarity and drains dry, and even
then the effect is modest. Treat it as a supporting measure, never the solution.

Diatomaceous earth is also documented ineffective here, both dry and moist,
because it absorbs moisture from the medium and loses its abrasive property.

## Biologicals, with efficacy

| Agent | Efficacy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *Steinernema feltiae* nematodes | 69-90% at 24 C; 81.6% lab | Strongest single purchase. Needs moist medium 24-48 h; repeat weekly x3 |
| *Stratiolaelaps scimitus* predatory mites | Excellent in trials | 6-8 weeks per application; apply twice a week apart |
| *Dalotia coriaria* rove beetles | Controls larvae | Slower to establish than mites |
| Bti | **Unreliable for Bradysia**: no effect on 2nd/3rd instars in lab, 1-51% field | Works on mosquitoes, not reliably on sciarids. Do not count on it |
| Azadirachtin | Alternative above 28 C | Where nematodes underperform in heat |

Product claims of "barrier" or "BTI kills gnats" are common in retail copy and
contradicted by the trial literature; cite the trial, not the product page.

## The bait-slice assay

Cheap census of the feeding stage, and the only way to separate a failing
treatment from a population emerging from pre-treatment pupae.

1. Raw potato slice ~5 mm thick, or a plug pushed into the medium.
2. Cut side down on the surface, pressed into contact.
3. Leave 24-48 h maximum (longer rots and moulds).
4. Count larvae underneath and in the soil contact layer.
5. Repeat weekly, several pots, fixed positions, write the number down.

Read it as an index, not a census: compare the same pot week to week, never one
pot to another. Falling toward zero across two checks is the success criterion.

Known failure modes: dry medium drives larvae deep and gives a false zero;
under a grit cap larvae cannot reach the slice at all; wet medium rots it.
Dispose of the slice with the larvae on it rather than shaking it back.

## Pantry pests (quick differential)

Larvae in a dry cupboard are **not** fungus gnats, which need constant moisture
and die in dry drawers.

| Sign | Culprit |
|---|---|
| Hairy, banded larva; shed skins ("husks") accumulating | Dermestid / carpet beetle |
| Small silk case dragged around | Case-bearing clothes moth |
| Silk webbing over flour; larvae climbing to pupate in corners | Indian meal moth |
| No webbing; small reddish-brown beetles in the bag | *Tribolium* flour beetle |
| Round holes punched through packaging | Drugstore / cigarette beetle |

A population surviving years without any infested bag is living on crumbs and
flour dust in seams, not on a source bag. Fix is vacuuming every seam including
drawer runners, sealing flour in glass, and freezing new flour 48 h before
storage. Pesticides are not needed and are the wrong tool.

## Measurement discipline

The user will sometimes propose skipping measurement because "the techniques are
proven". Restate that proven means 70-90%, and that the cheap assay costs one
potato and five minutes a week. An experiment that produces an impression rather
than a number has not been run.
